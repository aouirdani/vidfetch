Resolution tells you the dimensions of a video frame. In common 16:9 delivery, 720p is 1280 × 720 pixels and 1080p is 1920 × 1080. Consumer 4K usually means UHD at 3840 × 2160. Those numbers describe image dimensions, not a guarantee of sharpness, smooth motion or good compression.

## Read the complete label

The number in 720p or 1080p normally refers to the frame's vertical dimension, and the p means progressive scanning. A label such as 1080p60 also gives a frame rate: 60 frames per second. Frame rate and resolution describe different things.

Vertical phone recordings and other aspect ratios do not necessarily have the familiar landscape width. Look at both dimensions. A portrait clip can be tall without being a landscape 1920 × 1080 frame.

## UHD and cinema 4K are different

UHD's 3840 × 2160 frame has four times as many pixels as a 1920 × 1080 frame. Digital cinema can use a wider 4096-pixel frame. Calling both 4K is common, but their dimensions and aspect ratios differ.

When selecting a format, prefer explicit width and height over an ambiguous label. Cropping, rotation and a player's scaling can also affect the displayed picture.

## More pixels need adequate source detail

A higher-resolution file can retain finer detail when the original capture and encoding support it. It can also be an enlarged low-resolution source, or a heavily compressed rendition with visible blocks. Resolution alone cannot tell you which.

A 1080p copy with enough data for its scene may look cleaner than a poorly encoded 4K copy. Focus, camera noise and source quality matter before any download takes place. Read [bitrate versus resolution](/blog/bitrate-vs-resolution) to see why the two labels should be considered together.

## Choose according to how you will watch

For a small-screen reference copy, 720p may be sufficient. For a larger display or detailed text, 1080p or a higher available source may be useful. These are practical starting points, not fixed rules: viewing distance, content and device support change the answer.

If you are archiving your own work, keep the original rather than choosing only for today's phone. A smaller delivery version can be made later. If storage or transfer size is the priority, compare [ways to reduce size](/blog/reduce-video-size) before making a permanent choice.

## Using the format list in VidFetch

Only resolutions returned by the accessible source can be selected. VidFetch does not create a genuine 4K version from a lower-resolution video. Some formats contain separate video and audio streams; preparing a playable file can require combining those tracks.

A platform may refuse extraction, even when its page can be viewed in a browser. Availability is separate from resolution and is explained in [the troubleshooting guide](/blog/video-download-fails).

## Common question

### Will choosing 4K always use four times the storage?

No. Four times the pixels does not require exactly four times the bytes. Codec, bitrate, frame rate, scene complexity and duration also determine size.
